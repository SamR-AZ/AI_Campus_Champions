**Module 6 Reading 1: Cultivating Communities of Practice**

**Guided reading questions**

**_Q1: What are the three structural elements of a community of practice? Using these three elements, describe whether the AI Campus Champions workshop cohort currently meets the definition of a CoP, or falls short on one or more elements._**

Domain (shared area of knowledge/concern), community (relationships that enable learning), and practice (shared repertoire of tools, methods, stories). The cohort has a real domain and a forming community, but practice is thin and participation assigned; it is not yet a CoP unless these outlive Week 6.

**_Q2: Wenger et al. distinguish between 'core members,' 'active members,' and 'peripheral members.' How should an AI Campus Champion initiative accommodate all three participation levels without requiring everyone to become a core member?_**

Design so every level counts as full membership: public events anyone can watch, semiprivate "benches" (one-on-one channels) for peripheral members, and small time-boxed leadership roles for active ones. Cores are only 10-15% of a community; expect movement between levels rather than forcing it.

**_Q3: The book argues that CoPs cannot be mandated, only cultivated. What are the implications for how an AI Campus Champion at a university should propose an AI literacy initiative to leadership? What language would create space for cultivation rather than imposing a mandate?_**

Ask leadership for conditions, not a mandate: time, a coordinator, space, legitimacy, voluntary membership, since design should "bring out the community's own internal direction, character, and energy". Say "pilot," "invite," "support what is forming," not "require," "roll out," or attendance targets.

**_Q4: Wenger et al. identify 'knowledge stewarding' as a key CoP function. What knowledge management structures (shared resources, meeting rhythms, documentation practices) would an AI Campus Champion network need to function as a genuine CoP?_**

A named coordinator who also "works" the private space between meetings; a steady rhythm (biweekly meetings plus a forum), since rhythm is "the strongest indicator of its aliveness"; a searchable shared repository of prompts, workflows, and verified tips; and routine "what value did we get" check-ins.

**Key terms (my own definitions)**

| **Term**                                | **My definition (in my own words)**                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Community of practice**               | A voluntary group that keeps learning a shared topic together because membership pays off, not because it was assigned.     |
| **Domain (CoP)**                        | The shared area of knowledge or concern that defines what the group cares about and what counts as relevant.                |
| **Community (CoP)**                     | The people and the trust between them that make asking, sharing, and learning from each other possible.                     |
| **Practice (CoP)**                      | The group's working toolbox: methods, templates, stories, and shortcuts that members build and reuse.                       |
| **Knowledge stewardship**               | Actively curating the group's knowledge (documenting, organizing, connecting people to it) so it stays findable and usable. |
| **Legitimate peripheral participation** | Learning by taking part from the edges in low-risk ways before gradually moving toward the core.                            |
