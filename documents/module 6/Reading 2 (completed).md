**_Q2: The original taxonomy used nouns (Knowledge, Comprehension...); Anderson & Krathwohl revised it to verbs (Remember, Understand...). Why does the verb formulation produce more measurable learning objectives?_**

An objective pairs a noun (content) with a verb (process); the verb names an observable performance. "Comprehension" is a state you must infer; "compare" or "critique" is evidence a student produces or does not. Category names were "changed to verb form to fit the way they are used in objectives".

**_Q3: Anderson & Krathwohl add a fourth knowledge type, Metacognitive Knowledge. What is it? Give one example of a learning outcome in your professional domain that targets metacognitive knowledge._**

It is "knowledge about cognition in general as well as awareness of and knowledge about one's own cognition". Example from my lab: a grad student names the analysis steps they tend to over-trust with AI and writes a personal verification rule before results enter a manuscript.

**_Q4: When designing an AI literacy learning experience for your colleagues, at which Bloom's level would you start, and why? What evidence from this workshop's design would you cite to justify your choice?_**

Start at Remember/Understand: vocabulary for what LLMs are and cannot do before Apply-level tool training. Evidence: the workshop itself opened with Module 1 definitions and a concept map before tool work, and my colleagues' mixed fluency makes the base unskippable.

**Key terms (my own definitions)**

| **Term**                          | **My definition (in my own words)**                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Cognitive process dimension**   | The six verb families (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create) describing what learners do with content. |
| **Knowledge dimension**           | The four kinds of knowledge being worked on: factual, conceptual, procedural, and metacognitive.                         |
| **Learning objective verb**       | The action word in an objective that pins down the observable cognitive process expected (classify, critique, design).   |
| **Metacognitive knowledge**       | Knowing how thinking works, including your own habits, strengths, and blind spots.                                       |
| **Bloom's Revised Taxonomy**      | The two-dimensional grid (knowledge types x cognitive processes) used to write and classify learning objectives.         |
| **Measurable learning objective** | A verb-plus-noun statement specific enough that you can collect evidence of whether it was met.                          |
