**Task 1: Comparative Policy Analysis - Three Peer Institution Policies**

**Note:** The course platform policy library was not accessible, so three publicly posted AI policies from public R1 peers of the University of Arizona were used instead (documents fetched and read 10 July 2026; URLs in Sources). Scores follow the six-dimension rubric, 1-5.

**INSTITUTION A: Arizona State University (public R1, in-state peer) - distributed website guidance (Provost "Teaching and learning with generative AI" + "Digital Trust" guidelines); not an adopted policy; undated.**

| **Dimension**            | **Score** | **Justification (one sentence)**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------ | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 - SCOPE                | 2         | Students, faculty, and staff are addressed only implicitly through page sections; there is no applicability clause, no mention of contractors, and no statement that compliance is mandatory for anyone.                                                                         |
| 2 - CLARITY              | 3         | The Digital Trust rules are decision-usable for data ("Do not share information that is internal, sensitive or highly sensitive"), but all academic permissibility is deferred to individual instructors, so the university documents alone cannot answer a coursework question. |
| 3 - ENFORCEMENT          | 1         | No responsible office, reporting process, or consequence is named for AI misuse; enforcement defers to the pre-existing academic integrity apparatus, which the Provost's Office says it is still "currently reviewing" for generative AI.                                       |
| 4 - STUDENT PROTECTIONS  | 2         | FERPA-compliant contracted tools address data privacy, but the guidance is silent on AI detection, accusation procedures, and any student transparency or appeal rights.                                                                                                         |
| 5 - FACULTY/STAFF RIGHTS | 3         | Instructor authority over class-level AI rules is operative (per-class policies, an explicit refusal to ban AI, syllabus templates), but no academic-freedom guarantee is stated, and staff rights go unaddressed.                                                               |
| 6 - UPDATE CADENCE       | 1         | No page carries a publication date, version, or review schedule; the only commitment is the open-ended "this website will be updated."                                                                                                                                           |
| TOTAL                    | 12/30     | Sensible data rules resting on guidance with no owner, no dates, and no teeth.                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

**INSTITUTION B: University of Michigan (public R1, AAU peer) - website guidance (ITS Safe Computing "Artificial Intelligence and U-M Data" + genai.umich.edu suite) resting on pre-existing SPG policies; undated.**

| **Dimension**            | **Score** | **Justification (one sentence)**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------ | --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 - SCOPE                | 3         | Faculty, staff, and students across all campuses and Michigan Medicine are explicitly covered, but "U-M community members" is undefined and contractors and affiliates are never mentioned.                                                                                  |
| 2 - CLARITY              | 4         | Data rules are tied to defined classification levels and are genuinely actionable uncontracted AI services may take LOW-classified data only, while U-M's own services are approved to Moderate including FERPA - though academic permissibility is devolved to instructors. |
| 3 - ENFORCEMENT          | 2         | Named offices exist to consult (ITS Information Assurance, the IT User Advocate), but the guidance states no sanctions and hands violations to pre-existing misconduct processes and SPGs.                                                                                   |
| 4 - STUDENT PROTECTIONS  | 4         | U-M affirmatively protects students by discouraging AI detectors ("U-M does not recommend the use of AI-detection technology at this time given their high error rate") and by not training models on user data, but formal accusation and appeal procedures are absent.     |
| 5 - FACULTY/STAFF RIGHTS | 4         | Instructor authority is explicit and structural ("Each instructor must clearly articulate their own expectations and policies for GenAI use"), though academic freedom is never invoked by name.                                                                             |
| 6 - UPDATE CADENCE       | 2         | The content is visibly maintained (current model names appear throughout), but no page shows a date, and no review schedule is stated anywhere.                                                                                                                              |
| TOTAL                    | 19/30     | The best data-classification clarity of the three, undercut by undated, unscheduled guidance.                                                                                                                                                                                |

**INSTITUTION C: Purdue University (public R1, land-grant peer) - adopted, numbered university policy "Artificial Intelligence Use" (VII.A.5), issued 1 Nov 2025, plus Provost teaching guidance.**

| **Dimension**            | **Score** | **Justification (one sentence)**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------ | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 - SCOPE                | 5         | The policy expressly covers "All Purdue persons, departments, units, contractors, consultants and campuses using AI" the only document of the three that reaches contractors and consultants.                                                                                                                 |
| 2 - CLARITY              | 4         | Binding rules are precise - "Sensitive and Restricted Data... must not be entered into AI tools without prior approval," an approved-tools list, and a bar on AI as "the sole factor" in personnel, award, or disciplinary decisions with classroom specifics deferred to Provost guidance.                   |
| 3 - ENFORCEMENT          | 3         | Responsible offices are named (Purdue Systems Security, the CISO) with monitoring and a mandatory duty to self-report data incidents, but the policy states no consequences for violators.                                                                                                                    |
| 4 - STUDENT PROTECTIONS  | 4         | The binding policy protects student data and bars AI-only disciplinary decisions, and Provost guidance adds detector skepticism ("very high false-positive rates") plus a commitment never to share student PII with third-party AI, but the procedural protections sit in advisory guidance, not the policy. |
| 5 - FACULTY/STAFF RIGHTS | 4         | The policy structurally defers classroom use to Provost guidance, which vests authority in instructors ("The instructor, therefore, should determine and inform students when the use of AI is authorized or unauthorized"), though academic freedom is never named.                                          |
| 6 - UPDATE CADENCE       | 4         | The policy is dated, shows a documented review three months after adoption ("February 10, 2026: Policy reviewed, no changes"), and sits in a published change-log system, but no fixed review interval or next-review date is stated.                                                                         |
| TOTAL                    | 24/30     | The only adopted, dated, owned policy of the three, still missing stated sanctions and a scheduled review.                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

**200-word synthesis - strongest policy and most common gap:**

Purdue is the strongest of the three, and the only one whose document is actually a policy. It alone has an applicability clause reaching contractors and consultants, binding data rules keyed to an approved-tools list, named responsible offices with a mandatory incident-reporting duty, a substantive bar on AI-only disciplinary decisions, and a dated, documented review history. Michigan ranks second on the strength of its data-classification clarity and its unusually protective refusal to endorse AI detectors; ASU trails because its guidance, though sensible, is undated, unowned, and unenforceable. The most common gap is enforcement: not one of the three states consequences for violations each defers to pre-existing conduct processes that predate generative AI. Nearly as common: no forward review date anywhere, and student due-process protections that exist only in advisory guidance, if at all. The pattern shows where the sector's consensus is and is not developed. All three converge on risk-based data classification rules and instructor authority over classroom use; none has settled sanctions, accusation procedures, or scheduled review. And all three delegate the question students most need answered is this use permitted? to individual syllabi, preserving instructor discretion at the price of campus-wide clarity. (196 words)
