**Analyst Summary & Escalation Notes**

**1\. Analytical summary**

Across ten prompts, most outputs showed no strong bias, and the model often self-corrected: it hedged the "typical farmer" toward global smallholders and cast the fieldwork scientist as a Latina woman. Where bias appeared, it entered through unsolicited framing rather than the core deliverable. The substance (strength of each recommendation, quality of each bio) was equivalent across names; differences showed up in add-on descriptors. The female-coded letter picked up communal language ("a pleasure to work with") while male and androgynous letters got agentic framing ("thinks like a scientist"). History and "leading-today" lists skewed male and Western. The most professionally consequential finding is the differential framing in letters and bios, because staff paste these directly into admissions, hiring, and promotion materials. A recurring pattern where a Black-coded scientist's bio alone is steered toward "diversity labor" can shape real evaluation outcomes, unlike the representation lists, which are lower-stakes.

**2\. Findings to escalate**

I would escalate one finding and one process concern. The finding: the race-linked differential in prompt 5, where only the Black-coded scientist's bio added "broadening participation in the environmental sciences." This touches documents adjacent to hiring and promotion, so it carries equal-employment and reputational exposure and belongs in front of IT governance and, if it replicates, institutional leadership. I would escalate it as "investigate," not "confirmed," because a single run cannot establish a systematic pattern. The process concern is broader and more important than any single result: staff are likely using general-purpose AI to draft consequential personnel documents, where subtle framing differences by gender or race can influence real decisions. I would recommend that governance require human review before AI-drafted evaluative documents are used, discourage pasting them in unedited, and commission a larger repeated-trial audit (n≥5, multiple names per group) before drawing firm conclusions. The representation-list skews (prompts 7-8) I would log and monitor rather than escalate, since they are lower-stakes and partly reflect genuine field history.
