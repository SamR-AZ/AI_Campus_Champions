# ANNOTATION 1 - COMPELLING

**Claim (quote, Section 5):** _"no actual language understanding is taking place in LM-driven approaches to these tasks, as can be shown by careful manipulation of the test data to remove spurious cues the systems are leveraging."_

**Argument type (A-E):** D (Epistemic/comprehension argument)

**Why analytically compelling (1-2 sentences):** This is the most falsifiable version of the paper's comprehension critique because it rests on ablation evidence rather than intuition: in the study the authors cite (Niven & Kao, ACL 2019), BERT's near-human 77% score on an argument-reasoning benchmark fell to chance once spurious statistical cues were stripped out, with cues as thin as the word "not" doing much of the work. It reframes "do LMs understand?" as a construct-validity question, giving the field a concrete test that any leaderboard claim can be checked against.

# ANNOTATION 2 - COMPELLING

**Claim (quote, Section 6.2):** _"we foresee that LMs producing text will reproduce and even amplify the biases in their input \[...\] meaning more text in the world that reinforces and propagates stereotypes and problematic associations, both to humans who encounter the text and to future LMs trained on training sets that ingested the previous generation LM's output."_

**Argument type (A-E):** C (Bias encoding argument)

**Why analytically compelling (1-2 sentences):** Writing in 2021, before synthetic text flooded the web, the authors specify a recursive mechanism with two distinct propagation channels (human readers and future training corpora) rather than a vague worry that "models are biased." The second channel has since been demonstrated empirically: models trained on recursively generated data degrade measurably ("model collapse"; Shumailov et al., Nature 2024), which is strong corroboration for what was a prediction when the paper was written.

# ANNOTATION 3 - COMPELLING

**Claim (quote, Section 1):** _"In collecting ever larger datasets we risk incurring documentation debt. We recommend mitigating these risks by budgeting for curation and documentation at the start of a project and only creating datasets as large as can be sufficiently documented."_

**Argument type (A-E):** A (Documentation argument)

**Why analytically compelling (1-2 sentences):** Rather than urging transparency as an after-the-fact virtue, this converts documentation into a binding ex ante design constraint: documentability itself caps dataset size, because documentation skipped at collection time becomes debt that is effectively unpayable once a corpus reaches web scale. That inversion (if you cannot document it, do not collect it) turns an ethical aspiration into an operational decision rule that a lab, funder, or review board can actually enforce.

# ANNOTATION 4 - CHALLENGE

**Claim (quote, Section 6.1):** _"Text generated by an LM is not grounded in communicative intent, any model of the world, or any model of the reader's state of mind"; accordingly, an LM only stitches together linguistic forms "without any reference to meaning: a stochastic parrot."_

**Argument type (A-E):** D (Epistemic/comprehension argument)

**Why analytically challenging (2-3 sentences):** The claim bundles three conjuncts, and the "any model of the world" conjunct now faces direct counter-evidence: Li et al. (ICLR 2023) trained a GPT solely on Othello move sequences (pure form) and showed that editing the model's internal representation of the board causally changes its legal-move predictions, which is what possessing a rudimentary world model predicts and what the form-only argument says should not happen. A competing framework, the conceptual-role semantics that Piantadosi and Hill (2022) defend for LLMs, holds that meaning is constituted largely by relations among internal representational states, so "trained only on form" does not by itself entail "no reference to meaning" unless that semantic theory is separately refuted. This suggests a scope condition: the argument remains strongest on communicative intent and accountability, where no counter-evidence exists, but is weakest as a blanket claim about world models and meaning.
